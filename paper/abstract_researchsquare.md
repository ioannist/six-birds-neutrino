Replacing the SPT-3G 2018 temperature and polarization likelihood with the later SPT-3G D1 release, which observes essentially the same sky, has been reported to tighten cosmological upper limits on the summed neutrino mass (arXiv:2601.16277). We first ask whether the two releases make mutually consistent predictions. Following the Six Birds framework, we treat each release as a "lens" and measure, in each direction, how much worse one release fits at parameters chosen by the other. We split the SPT quadratic part of this mismatch exactly over spectra and multipoles using the full covariance matrix.

At numerically optimized (not certified-global) fits, the mismatch is large and asymmetric: about 488 (D1 given 2018) versus 79 (2018 given D1) in chi-square units with SPT and DESI DR2 BAO, and about 63 versus 23 with a Planck subset added. Where it sits depends on baseline and direction: high-multipole temperature leads with SPT and DESI, while high-multipole polarization (EE) leads the D1-given-2018 direction once the Planck subset is included. Cutting D1 back to the 2018 multipole coverage, dropping the SPT optical-depth prior, or raising Boltzmann precision changes each tested total by less than 4 percent.

Our sampled neutrino-mass posteriors do not meet pre-declared convergence and precision criteria, and the likelihoods proved sensitive to solver version and numerical settings, so we report no magnitude for the tightening. A machine-checked (Lean 4) analysis of two Gaussian constraints with the physical gate (mass at least zero) shows that tension between two constraints that both prefer positive masses can never put the posterior mode at zero, and that tightening one constraint raises the chance of such a mode only when the other constraint's mean is positive.
