\section{Discussion, limitations, and next diagnostics}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What we learn from lens-swap auditing}
The inconsistency emphasized by \cite{arxiv2601_16277} is not merely a parameter-level curiosity; it is a stability question about packaged inference under a lens swap. In the Six Birds / SBT framing \cite{sixbirds_foundations,sixbirds_darkenergy}, the two SPT likelihood releases define two lenses, and the posterior for $\mnu$ is a packaged macro object $E=\Completion\circ\Lens$. Our audits operationalize the question ``are these packages mutually predictive?'' and the answer is: \emph{not reliably}.

Three takeaways are most relevant for interpreting $\mnu$ shifts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Mismatch is large and directional.} The held-out mismatch statistics $\dchi_{B|A}$ and $\dchi_{A|B}$ are both substantial and asymmetric (\cref{tab:audit-summary}), reinforcing that route mismatch is not captured by a single symmetric ``tension'' number.
  \item \textbf{Mismatch is localized in TT high-$\ell$ within the overlap region.} The localization heatmaps (\cref{fig:profiled-localization}) show that the dominant held-out failures arise from TT structure at high multipoles that both lenses cover. This supports treating the ``curious shift'' in \cite{arxiv2601_16277} as entangled with packaging differences in how the two releases compress and model the same sky information.
  \item \textbf{Common-staging does not resolve the mismatch.} Restricting D1 to the SPT2018 multipole support changes the profiled mismatch only at the few-percent level (\cref{fig:profiled-staging-compare}). This argues against a purely support/cut explanation and motivates reporting lens-swap audits alongside headline bounds when pipeline changes are in play.
\end{enumerate}

Together with the reproduced direction of the DESI-driven $\mnu$ tightening under SPT2018$\rightarrow$D1 (\cref{sec:results-mnu}), these results support a conservative reading: $\mnu$ shifts can be \emph{packaging-sensitive} whenever the packaged likelihood objects disagree in localized regions of the summary statistics.

\subsection{Limitations}
This note is deliberately scoped and has several limitations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Completion/nuisance choices.} Our production $\mnu$ chains use a minimal nuisance sampling set for computational tractability, and our profiled audits optimize only a runtime-capped subset of nuisance parameters (\cref{sec:profiled-audit}). Broader nuisance freedom and alternative priors can shift tight sub-0.1\,eV bounds.
  \item \textbf{Likelihood and baseline implementation details.} While we match the Planck-subset+PR4-lensing structure described in \cite{arxiv2601_16277}, small implementation choices (e.g.\ the low-$\ell$ EE variant available in a given likelihood wrapper) can propagate into tight derived bounds.
  \item \textbf{Finite compute and crude diagnostics.} Some of our MCMC diagnostics for $\mnu$ (ESS and split-$\hat R$ proxies \cite{gelman_rubin1992,vehtari2021_rhat}) are necessarily crude given the expense of CMB+Boltzmann runs. Our aim is to reproduce qualitative behavior and provide audit localization, not to provide definitive converged limits beyond what is needed for the lens-swap comparison.
  \item \textbf{Audits over likelihood objects, not raw maps.} Our analyses operate on released likelihood objects. As such, the audits identify where the packaged summaries disagree, but they do not by themselves pinpoint whether the origin is calibration, beam/transfer functions, foreground modeling, mapmaking, or other pipeline components.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Next diagnostics (and non-novelty acknowledgement)}
A decisive next step, emphasized already by \cite{arxiv2601_16277}, is to reprocess the 2018 raw data through the D1 pipeline. We explicitly \emph{do not} claim novelty for proposing this idea. Our contribution is to clarify why such a test is well-motivated in packaging terms and to provide a set of quantitative artifacts that can be used to evaluate it.

Beyond reprocessing, SBT suggests two complementary diagnostic directions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Cross-lens posterior predictive checks (PPD-style audits) \cite{gelman_meng_stern1996_ppc}.} Rather than comparing only best-fit points, one can fit $\theta$ under lens $A$ and forward-predict the summary statistics used by lens $B$ (and vice versa), quantifying discrepancies as a function of spectrum and multipole. This would turn the localization maps here into a full predictive audit over posterior uncertainty.
  \item \textbf{Minimal ``rewrite mode'' tests.} When mismatch localizes to specific blocks (e.g.\ TT high-$\ell$), one can introduce a small surrogate correction (a template mode or calibration mode) whose role is to reduce cross-lens mismatch without being interpreted as new physics. Success would be measured by improved cross-lens predictivity and increased stability of $\mnu$ under staging and dataset routing; failure would sharpen the case that a deeper inconsistency (or missing modeling) remains.
\end{enumerate}
Both directions are concrete and falsifiable and naturally complement standard parameter-tension narratives.

\subsection{Practical recommendation}
When pipeline/likelihood changes are known to move tight constraints, we recommend that headline parameter bounds (such as $\mnu$) be reported together with: (i) a directional cross-lens audit statistic (e.g.\ $\dchi_{B|A}$ and $\dchi_{A|B}$), (ii) a localization map by spectrum and multipole, and (iii) a common-staging control. These artifacts are small enough to be routine, yet they materially change how one should interpret shifts in packaged constraints under lens swaps.
